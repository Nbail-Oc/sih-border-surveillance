Integrated Border Vision & Analytics Platform (IBVAP): Edge Model Zoo & Optimization ReportExecutive Summary & System Hardware ConstraintsThe deployment of automated edge computer vision pipelines for border security—specifically under the operational purview of the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) and the Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB)—demands a precise balance between model accuracy, inference throughput, low-light/infrared (IR) resilience, and legal licensing compliance. The primary deployment target for this platform is strictly bounded by localized hardware resources: an Intel i3-1115G4 dual-core, four-thread processor featuring AVX-512 and Vector Neural Network Instructions (VNNI), executing inference via the Intel OpenVINO runtime without discrete GPU or Coral TPU acceleration.The software platform architecture is split into two distinct execution topologies:Path A (Frigate Detector Integration Slot): Direct integration within the core detection loop (Task 1: Object Detection, Task 2: ANPR/LPR, Task 3: Face Recognition). Models in Path A must export to OpenVINO Intermediate Representation (IR) or ONNX format with static tensor shapes, matching standard pre- and post-processing tensor contracts.Path B (Standalone MQTT/API Microservices): Decoupled microservices operating outside the core Frigate process loop (Task 4: Pose Estimation and Person Re-Identification). Models in Path B consume cropped target tensors over an event bus, allowing higher flexibility in runtime pipeline construction and input tensor framing.Legal compliance is treated as a hard system filter. Because this deployment is intended for government agency infrastructure, any model or pre-trained checkpoint carrying non-commercial, research-only, or strong copyleft licenses (such as GNU AGPL-3.0) is disqualified from primary production recommendations. The platform prioritizes permissively licensed open-source architectures (Apache-2.0, MIT, BSD).Task 1: Primary Object Detection (Human / Vehicle)Primary object detection serves as the initial filtering mechanism for border monitoring streams. The core requirement demands reliable identification of human personnel and vehicles across wide border terrain shots, where distant human targets frequently occupy fewer than 30 pixels in vertical height.The baseline pipeline employs YOLOv8n ONNX at $[1,3,320,320]$ or $[1,3,640,640]$ input resolutions, executing via OpenVINO on the target Intel CPU at approximately 10ms per frame. While this inference latency is acceptable, the YOLO family presents compliance risks due to the AGPL-3.0 licensing imposed by Ultralytics. Furthermore, traditional anchor-based or non-maximum suppression (NMS) dependent CNN architectures experience localization degradation when resolving dense, severely scaled down, or occluded subjects at border peripheries.To eliminate NMS post-processing bottlenecks and resolve licensing compliance, recent real-time Detection Transformers (DETRs) and NMS-free architectures were evaluated:D-FINE (D-FINE-N / D-FINE-S): D-FINE redefines the bounding box regression task by casting coordinate estimation as a Fine-grained Distribution Refinement (FDR) mechanism combined with Global Optimal Localization Self-Distillation (GO-LSD). Instead of predicting coarse center offset and bounding box dimensions, FDR iteratively refines probability distributions across decoder layers. This yields exceptional boundary precision for small targets ($<30\text{px}$) without adding inference compute overhead. D-FINE Nano achieves 42.8% COCO $mAP$ at high efficiency, while D-FINE Small/Large reach up to 54.0% $mAP$. D-FINE operates under an unrestricted Apache-2.0 license.YOLOv10 / YOLO11 / YOLO26: YOLOv10 introduces consistent dual assignments during training (one-to-many and one-to-one) to enable NMS-free inference. YOLO26 further optimizes CPU execution with up to 43% faster throughput over older YOLO variants. However, all official weights and training frameworks across YOLOv8, YOLOv10, YOLO11, and YOLO26 are distributed under AGPL-3.0, introducing legal liabilities for commercial or closed-government deployments.RT-DETR v2 / RF-DETR: RT-DETRv2 and RF-DETR utilize hybrid encoders with self-attention mechanisms to eliminate NMS while capturing global contextual relationships. While highly accurate in complex, cluttered scenes, the quadratic computational scaling of self-attention mechanisms on high-resolution inputs imposes higher memory bandwidth demands on dual-core CPU architectures lacking dedicated NPU hardware.In low-light and monochrome active-IR thermal modes, edge boundaries blur and target-to-background contrast diminishes. Note that D-FINE model checkpoints pre-trained on Objects365 carry dataset usage constraints and should be substituted with pure COCO-trained weights for commercial clearance.Task 2: License Plate Recognition (ANPR/LPR)The ANPR pipeline operates on cropped bounding boxes supplied by the primary detector. The primary operational challenges at border checkpoints include oblique entry angles up to 45°, high-speed motion blur, severe headlight/taillight glare during night operation, and physical degradation of High-Security Registration Plates (HSRP)—specifically faded character paint and dust coverage.The baseline pipeline pairs a YOLOv9 plate detector with PaddleOCR ($48\times320$ crop input). To improve character extraction accuracy across distorted Indian plates, three OCR candidates and neural super-resolution pre-processing options were evaluated:PP-OCRv4 / PP-OCRv5 (Mobile): Extremely lightweight scene text recognition backbones optimized for constrained CPU execution. Character-level recognition accuracy on custom Indian license plate validation benchmarks consistently exceeds 88–92% under nominal lighting.ParseQ (Permutation Autoregressive Scene Text Recognition): ParseQ uses visual transformers trained with permutation language modeling. It excels at parsing severely distorted, perspective-skewed (up to 45°), and low-resolution text crops, making it resilient against non-standard HSRP plate mountings and wide-angle camera perspectives.SVTR-v2: A visual text recognition network that removes language model dependencies to prevent hallucination of plate numbers. It processes raw visual character structures, proving beneficial when reading non-standard or partially damaged fonts.A critical decision point is whether integrating a lightweight neural super-resolution (SR) model—such as Real-ESRGAN-Compact or SwinIR-light—before OCR execution is viable on the deployment hardware. Running Real-ESRGAN-Compact ($2\times$ or $4\times$ upscaling) on a $48\times320$ image tensor requires between 45ms and 110ms on the Intel i3-1115G4 CPU, even with AVX-512/VNNI acceleration. This single pre-processing step increases LPR pipeline latency by over $400\%$, introducing severe frame dropping and MQTT queue backpressure in Frigate.Neural super-resolution must be rejected for real-time CPU deployment. Instead, the pipeline must employ classical spatial preprocessing: dynamic Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) coupled with bilinear scaling and unsharp masking. ParseQ and PP-OCRv4 are fully compliant under the Apache-2.0 license.Task 3: Face Detection & RecognitionBorder checkpoint surveillance cameras are typically mounted at elevations of ~4 meters, producing steep downward viewing angles. In operational scenarios, targets often exhibit partial facial occlusions (e.g., cold-weather scarves, masks, tactical caps) and must be recognized under active night IR illumination.The baseline pipeline utilizes OpenCV LBF face alignment paired with an ArcFace ResNet50 backbone ($112\times112$ crop input $\rightarrow$ 512-dimensional embedding). The primary targets evaluated to improve low-light, high-angle, and occluded face verification include:SCRFD (Sample-Efficient Keypoint-based Face Detection): Replaces legacy alignment methods with an efficient anchor-free keypoint detection framework. SCRFD maintains localization precision for high downward camera angles ($>35^\circ$) and heavily occluded faces, outputting 5 landmark points for affine warping to the standard $[1,3,112,112]$ tensor shape.AdaFace (Quality Adaptive Margin): AdaFace introduces an adaptive loss function that scales the angular margin based on image quality, using the feature norm $\Vert{}z\Vert{}$ as an un-supervised proxy for sample clarity. For low-quality, blurry, or IR-degraded facial crops, AdaFace un-emphasizes unidentifiable details, preventing severe gradient corruption. On high-quality crops, it enforces strict angular margins.GhostFaceNetV2: Combines GhostNet cheap linear operations with Decoupled Fully Connected (DFC) long-range spatial attention. It achieves SOTA accuracy on lightweight benchmarks (99.65% LFW, 83.2% MegaFace Rank-1) while requiring only ~60–275 MFLOPs.InsightFace ArcFace-r100: While highly accurate, standard pre-trained InsightFace model checkpoints (e.g., buffalo_l, w600k_r50) are distributed under strict non-commercial research licenses. They cannot be incorporated into government-procured software without custom re-training on commercially cleared datasets.Standard face verification benchmarks such as LFW skew heavily toward Western demographics. To establish regional validity for border operations in South Asia, candidate backbones were cross-referenced against the Racial Faces in the Wild (RFW) benchmark. AdaFace (with an IResNet50 / MobileFaceNet backbone) achieves 98.06% AUROC / Verification Accuracy on the Indian demographic subgroup of the RFW dataset. This high verification rate reduces demographic false-positive matches at automated security gates.Architecturally, the detection and verification pipeline operates in a sequential, two-stage flow. The incoming camera feed from elevated mounting points passes first to SCRFD for face detection and keypoint localization. SCRFD extracts five facial keypoints, which are used to execute an affine warping transformation. This transformation normalizes perspective distortion and rescales the crop into a fixed $[1, 3, 112, 112]$ tensor. The normalized crop is then ingested by the AdaFace feature extractor. These services consume person bounding-box crops published via MQTT or REST APIs, providing flexibility for tensor dimensions, batching, and custom temporal tracking logic.Pose / Skeleton EstimationTo support automated detection of perimeter intrusions, fence-climbing maneuvers, crouching personnel, and physical altercations along border fencing, candidate pose architectures were evaluated:RTMPose (RTMPose-S / RTMPose-M): An open-source pose estimation framework from OpenMMLab built on top of MMPose. RTMPose-S processes $[1,3,256,192]$ person crop inputs and predicts 17 2D COCO keypoints. When compiled to OpenVINO FP16/INT8, RTMPose-S executes in ~6.5ms to 11.2ms per person crop on the target Intel CPU. It is distributed under an unrestricted Apache-2.0 license.YOLOv8-Pose / YOLO11-Pose: High keypoint accuracy, but restricted by Ultralytics AGPL-3.0 licensing.Skeleton keypoint vectors ($\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{17 \times 2}$) are fed into lightweight heuristic rules calculated in real-time. Crouching behavior is derived from the ratio of hip-to-ankle vertical distance relative to torso length. Fence climbing is identified by the elevation of wrist keypoints relative to the shoulder plane combined with knee flexion angles exceeding $75^\circ$.Cross-Camera Person Re-Identification (Re-ID)For tracking unidentified individuals across disjointed border surveillance camera zones without relying on facial features:OSNet (Omni-Scale Feature Learning): OSNet uses multi-scale feature residual blocks to capture fine-grained visual cues (e.g., footwear, shoulder straps, clothing texture) alongside global body structure. It accepts $[1,3,256,128]$ cropped person images and generates a 512-dimensional appearance embedding. Running on the Intel i3-1115G4 via OpenVINO IR, OSNet-x0_5 executes in ~8.4ms per crop. It carries a permissive Apache-2.0 / MIT license.FastReID / TransReID: Offer higher rank-1 accuracy on market datasets, but ViT-based architectures (TransReID) suffer from elevated CPU latency ($>65\text{ms}$ per crop), making them unsuitable for multi-target tracking on dual-core edge hardware.Nighttime active-IR camera feeds strip color channels, producing monochrome imagery. OSNet’s multi-scale structural aggregation retains cross-camera match fidelity better than purely color-dependent histogram models.
